# WPMediaVerse Layout Modes

**Five ways to display your media library. Choose the one that matches your community — or switch between them as your needs change.**

---

## Why Layout Matters More Than You Think

Layout is not decoration. It is the first thing a new visitor uses to decide whether this platform is for people like them.

A flat grid says "this is a library." A card feed says "this is a social network." A masonry wall says "this is for discovery and inspiration." A photo gallery with EXIF data says "serious photographers live here."

Every other WordPress media plugin gives you one layout and expects you to live with it. WPMediaVerse gives you five, tied to the same data — switching is a setting change, not a migration.

---

## Grid Layout (Free)

**The universal default.**

Clean, even columns. Every image occupies the same amount of space. No hierarchy imposed by image orientation or content type.

Use grid when your audience wants to scan a library efficiently. It is neutral, fast to build familiarity with, and works for any type of content. This is the layout most communities start with.

**Works best for:**
- General-purpose community media pages
- Sites where members upload a variety of content types and formats
- Any site that needs a professional baseline without a specific visual personality

**Included in the free version.**

## Instagram Feed (Pro)

**Social-first. Engagement-first.**

Vertical card feed with each photo getting its own card. User avatar, display name, and timestamp appear at the top of each card. Reactions, comment preview, and share are at the bottom. The photo fills the middle.

This layout is about the social layer, not just the content. The card format signals: this is a place where people share moments, react to each other's work, and have conversations around images.

**Why the card format drives engagement:** The reaction row is always visible — members do not need to open the lightbox to react. This dramatically lowers the friction of engagement. Views-to-reactions ratios are consistently higher on card-based feeds than on grid layouts where interactions are hidden behind a click.

**Works best for:**
- Community-driven platforms where social interaction is as important as the content itself
- Sites migrating from Instagram-like platforms that want a familiar experience
- BuddyPress communities where the activity feed model is already familiar to members

---

## Pinterest / Masonry (Pro)

**Discovery-first. Browsing-first.**

Variable-height columns that fill naturally based on each image's proportions. Tall images are tall. Wide images are wide. The page feels dense with content without feeling cluttered.

Masonry is the layout for browsing. There is no next page break, no start and end — the page keeps offering content as you scroll. This makes it ideal for communities where discovery is the main activity: finding new photographers, new styles, new ideas.

**The Pinterest effect:** Masonry layouts are associated with "saving" and "collecting" because they originate from Pinterest. Members in masonry-layout communities tend to use favorites and bookmarks more than members on grid or card layouts. If you want to build a collection-oriented community, this layout reinforces that behavior.

**Works best for:**
- Inspiration boards and creative discovery communities
- Interior design, fashion, travel, and lifestyle communities
- Sites where content is mixed-format (portraits alongside landscapes alongside macro shots)
- Communities where "I'll come back to this later" is a primary member behavior

---

## Flickr Gallery (Pro)

**Craft-first. Detail-first.**

Uniform grid with a detail-oriented lightbox. When a member opens a photo, they get the full-resolution image, EXIF data (camera make and model, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal length), technical metadata, and download options.

The Flickr layout signals to photographers that this is a platform that respects their work at a technical level. It is not a social feed with photos in it — it is a photo gallery with a community layer.

**EXIF display:** WPMediaVerse reads EXIF data at upload time and stores it in `mvs_media_meta`. In the Flickr layout, this data is surfaced in the lightbox detail panel alongside the image. Photographers who care about gear and technique find this information genuinely useful.

**Works best for:**
- Photography communities where the craft matters, not just the aesthetics
- Fine art photography platforms
- Camera club and enthusiast communities
- Any community where members want to learn from each other's technical decisions

---

## Dribbble Portfolio (Pro)

**Work-first. Clean.**

Minimal presentation. The image is the center of attention. Social elements are present but subdued — available when you look for them, not competing with the work for visual attention.

The Dribbble layout is designed for people who want their portfolio to stand on its own. It removes the "this is a social platform" feel and replaces it with "this is a showcase." Members feel like they are presenting work, not posting content.

**The key difference from other layouts:** In most layouts, the UI announces itself — avatars, reaction rows, comment counts, and timestamps are always visible. In Dribbble layout, the work is the announcement. Metadata and interactions are accessible but require intentional action to surface.

**Works best for:**
- Designer and creative professional communities
- Portfolio showcase platforms
- Studios and agencies building a shared work showcase for their team
- Communities where members are more sensitive about the presentation quality of their work

## Switching Between Layouts

Changing layout mode is a one-setting change in the WPMediaVerse admin panel. No data migration is required. The same photos, albums, reactions, and comments are displayed differently based on the selected layout.

You can also set different layouts for different pages. The explore feed can use Instagram mode. A specific member's public profile page can use Flickr mode. A featured gallery page can use Dribbble mode.

## Which Layout Should You Choose?

If you are unsure where to start:

- **Starting a new community without a clear visual identity?** Start with Grid. Switch once you understand what your members upload and how they browse.
- **Your members are social-first and came from Instagram or Facebook?** Instagram Feed.
- **Your members browse for inspiration more than they share their own work?** Pinterest / Masonry.
- **Your members are serious photographers who care about gear and technique?** Flickr Gallery.
- **Your members are designers or creatives who want their work taken seriously?** Dribbble.

All five are included in Pro. You are not locked into one.
