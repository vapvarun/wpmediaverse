## Why this is not a green pass

The walk ran in two parts and neither part covered the whole runbook.

The dispatched worker had **no Playwright and no Local-WP MCP tools** in its session. It substituted curl (with real auth cookies) plus direct MySQL, which proves the server-rendered half honestly and proves nothing about client-side behaviour. It marked roughly half the runbook ⚠️ HUMAN rather than assuming passes — the right call, and the reason this report exists instead of a JSON.

The reviewer (this pass) then executed the interactive checks the worker could not. That closed the highest-value client-side gaps but not the ones needing real uploads, a second live user, or another browser.

**No `.last-smoke-pass.json` was written.** Per the skill contract, an "almost green" artifact defeats the gate's whole purpose. `bin/build-release.sh` will therefore refuse to package, which is the correct outcome.

Correction to an earlier claim in this session: I asserted that `build-release.sh` has no
freshness check and that the stale 2026-08-31 report would therefore have passed. **That was
wrong.** The check exists, was added 2026-08-19 after the 2.4.0 cycle hit this exact failure, and
it fires correctly here. Verified by running its own comparison:

    smoke report ran_at     2026-09-01 01:52:42  (1788207762)
    newest source commit    2026-09-02 11:55:29  (1788330329)
    report <= last commit   -> STALE -> exit 30

There is also a second guard requiring `C_core_flows.pass >= 10`, on the reasoning that "zero
failures is not evidence when nothing ran". Both guards are working as designed. The release is
blocked by a gate doing its job, not by a gap in one.
